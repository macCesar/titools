# Audio pipeline correction

Version 1.0.2 removes final-output retention on Android and iOS while retaining one stereo output per platform, fixed event gains, waveform profiles and chord compensation. It adds no public options. Existing envelopes, release tails and parameter transitions keep their roles.

The final protector responds immediately to each stereo sample peak. This removes the old mandatory 10 ms delay, at the cost of a different overload response. Heavy mixes can audibly change waveform shape. Protection recovers with a 100 ms exponential time constant. The 0.85/0.70 ceilings describe sample values, not inter-sample peaks or speaker behavior. The master now follows the same linear 10 ms ramp on both platforms. iOS consumers requesting `raw` now receive its documented gain instead of the conservative gain; review those mixes.

`tests/output_core_regression.c` checks linked impulses, silence/reset, bounded recovery and exact/retargeted master ramps at 16, 44.1, 48, 96 and 192 kHz (absolute double tolerance 1e-12). Android host checks compile the real C++ callback. Apple checks extract the actual callback, helpers and start validation/profile selection, replacing device/Titanium plumbing. The historical Java DSP model is not an oracle for native sound. CI runs production output checks alongside transport, effects, spatial and statistics regressions.

`tools/calibrate_studio.py` (Android by default, `--platform ios` for Apple) reports all 37 Studio presets individually across independent low/mid/high notes, grouped chords, unisons and overlapping releases at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 voices. Its recommendation uses the maximum measured pre-protection peak for the declared usual configuration (1-8 voices) to target −6 dBFS, with 0.01 dB measurement margin and the public volume limit. It reports 16/32-voice stress separately. The recommendations apply to the measured configuration. Event gains remain fixed, and other workloads can exceed the target. Effects and new LFOs are disabled in preset calibration. It never edits Studio.

The before/after host comparison covered 80 PCM pairs across both renderers at five sample rates. After aligning the former output delay, all 40 cases without overload preserved the waveform within an absolute float tolerance of 1e-7. Overload cases remained finite and within the sample ceiling; their waveform differences were measured. Matched host benchmarks found no median callback-time increase. These measurements do not establish whole-app CPU use or physical output latency.

Physical comparisons used Studio and Frontier on an OPPO CPH2639 running Android 16 and an iPad. Studio checks covered its 37 presets, short notes, Hold, chords, repetitions, sequences, effects and spatial controls. Frontier checks covered gameplay with simultaneous sounds. The same native candidate binaries were then integrated into the real Studio and Frontier apps, which built and launched on both devices. Their presets and gains were preserved. Route-wide latency, Bluetooth behavior and extended maximum-load acceptance remain outside these results.

Android can report an underrun during stream startup. An initial matched repeat recorded it in four of six starts for both the original and candidate, with no additional underruns during the subsequent note/chord checks. Further silent tests reproduced the symptom with an independent Oboe 1.11.0 callback that only writes zeros. The startup condition remains unresolved; the counter is retained. Silent tests do not establish whether it causes an audible defect. Oboe's open [LatencyTuner proposal](https://github.com/google/oboe/pull/2432) discusses how startup XRuns affect buffer tuning, but does not eliminate their cause. TiSynthEngine does not use that tuner.

PCM, original and candidate packages, commands, hashes and device traces are retained outside the app repositories. The physical results apply to the tested devices and scenarios.
