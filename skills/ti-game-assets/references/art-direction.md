# Art contract and masters

Before drawing, read the project's workflow/inventory and inspect its actual reference images. Record style, palette (swatches where useful), materials, texture, perspective, orientation, silhouette, contrast and detail density for each set. A rusty scrap collection cannot dictate a clean spaceship or a different world's palette. Separate the visual style reference from a composition reference and from an edit target.

For raster-authored art, keep each individual HD PNG with real alpha in the source-of-truth directory, alongside prompt, generation/edit provenance and reference roles. Use `image_gen` for raster creation/edits; request `transparent_background: true` for cutouts. Inspect each result on light, dark and checkerboard backgrounds: a painted checkerboard is not transparency. Preserve authored SVG/procedural pipelines when the project uses them or the user requests them; read [vector-sources.md](vector-sources.md). Those scripts author editable geometry and render derivatives, rather than editing raster drawings. CLI/API generation is an explicitly chosen alternative, not an automatic fallback.

Gameplay sprites must have no cast, ground or exterior drop shadows, especially moving/rotating objects. Internal shading is allowed. Declare intentional light emission or halos as effects so alpha cleanup preserves them. Background art can have lighting appropriate to its composition.

## Canvas use and HD detail

Generate each individual sprite as a separate transparent HD master, using as much of the available canvas as its complete silhouette permits. Request tight framing at generation time, an appropriate aspect ratio when supported, and only the narrow transparent edge needed to retain the entire outline and declared effects. Do not default to 78%, 80%, generous margins or a fixed padding percentage. Irregular shapes naturally leave transparent areas; maximum canvas use does not mean distorting or clipping them to fill every pixel.

Inspect the current tool's capabilities and each output's actual dimensions. Outputs observed around 1254×1254 or nearly 1400 pixels wide are observations, not guaranteed sizes or resolution ceilings. Request the highest suitable supported generation quality and retain the original pixels. A sprite displayed at 32×32 still deserves a detailed HD source; native sizes, atlas cells and density exports do not set master resolution.

For HD art, preserve the world's crisp geometry, clean contours, textures, material reflections, controlled gradients and internal shading. Review both full resolution and intended display sizes. A larger file alone does not establish quality. Alpha-tight cropping may remove residual empty space without resampling; it neither creates missing detail nor substitutes for generating a well-framed subject. If excessive margins leave the subject too small or poorly defined, regenerate it. Do not upscale a small drawing and call it an HD master.

Specify the required finish in the generation prompt, not merely "HD" or a large pixel size: finely resolved material textures and joints, clean continuous gradients without banding or warped shading, precise straight lines, smooth regular curves, clearly delineated forms and crisp contours. Reflections, bevels, highlights and shadows must describe the object's structure coherently. Preserve the intended texture density rather than adding random detail to fill space. Review these features at native master resolution and after reduction to every relevant display size; regenerate defects in the source instead of relying on sharpening or downscaling to hide them. Pixel art follows its logical-block contract below rather than these continuous-gradient requirements.

When the user requests reconstruction of manufactured geometry, distinguish recognizable design/topology from defects in the reference rendering. Use the original as a design reference rather than preserving warped contours, uneven rings, wavy gradients or smeared highlights as invariants. Define the required straight panel edges, regular curves, concentric rings, bevels and mechanical joints explicitly. Require geometric symmetry only where the design calls for it; if matching illumination is also requested, specify balanced lighting and corresponding highlights/shadows. Review the result against the stated improvement: a near-identical copy with minor sharpening does not satisfy a request for substantial reconstruction. Preserve deliberate asymmetry and other styles when their contract requires them.

Keep documented common canvases, scale and pivots for intact/damaged states and animation sequences. Explosions reserve the maximum intended radius across frames; propulsion reserves its declared flame extent; asteroid sizes follow the world's variant contract. These technical needs can require empty space. There is no universal occupancy percentage by category. Record any deliberate margin with its reason, actual generation dimensions and subsequent crop offsets. Repeating textures and backgrounds follow their edge-continuity/composition contracts rather than individual-sprite cropping.

## Pixel art

Declare a logical size, typically 32×32, 64×64, 128×128 or 256×256; justify other sizes. Require a strict grid with proportional pixel blocks, consistent block size, constrained palette and no subpixel edge shading. Keep the HD master. Prepare the logical output with nearest-neighbor resampling, then inspect palette and borders at integer zoom: nearest-neighbor resizing alone cannot repair a generator's inconsistent grid. Correct the drawing when necessary. Inspect current image-generator capabilities before choosing master size; no fixed tool-resolution maximum belongs in this contract.

Define block size from the actual final presentation, not from the HD file's resolution or the phrase "pixel-art style". For example, a 32×32 impact and a 64×64 explosion need different logical grids: an HD master near 1280 pixels wide would represent each logical pixel with roughly 40 or 20 source pixels respectively. These are illustrative ratios, not fixed generator dimensions. Request uniform square blocks aligned to the logical grid, with flat color/alpha inside each block; prohibit tiny texture, subpixel sparks and mixed block sizes that disappear or blur after reduction.

Distinguish atlas cell size from visible phase extent. If a 64×64 cell shows a phase only 36 pixels wide, define the source-to-logical mapping for that 36-pixel visible extent or keep a registered 64×64 logical canvas whose artwork occupies 36 pixels. Do not independently maximize all frames and then resize them by different arbitrary factors: that changes apparent block size. Preserve the sequence's intended growth and pixel scale through the documented exporter. Compare states, frames and asteroid size variants at actual game size and integer nearest-neighbor zoom, not only as large HD previews.

Pixel-art smoke can use progressive partial alpha without becoming painted HD smoke. Use deliberate square clusters with controlled alpha levels, transparent gaps and lower-alpha outer clusters; each cluster remains flat RGBA on the same grid as the effect. Preserve those alpha levels during export. Do not replace the smoke with opaque grey polygons, apply binary-alpha cleanup, introduce blurred subpixel fringes or resample pixel art with smoothing. HD effects instead use continuous soft alpha and their declared HD filtering.

## States and sequences

Maintain silhouette, scale, orientation, canvas and pivot between intact/damaged variants. An explosion may grow; reserve its maximum canvas and keep the same anchor throughout. Review contact sheets and a playback preview for animation continuity, frame order, lighting drift and generator deviations. Correct deviations before integration, rather than hiding them in changing hitboxes.

Cover the families the world needs: players, enemies, allies, obstacles, ammunition, rewards, ambient objects and backgrounds. Declare omissions intentionally in the contract; no universal file count applies to every world.
